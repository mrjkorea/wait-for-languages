A, An, The: A Simple Guide to English Articles for Korean Speakers

By Mr. Jay, an English teacher in Korea

Ask any English teacher in Korea which grammar point causes the most trouble, and many will say articles: a, an, and the. That makes sense. Korean doesn’t have articles, so there is nothing to translate. The good news is that you don’t need to memorize dozens of rules. A few simple questions will get you right most of the time.

Why articles feel strange for Korean speakers

In Korean, “책 읽었어” works whether you mean a book, the book, or books in general. Context does the work. English puts that information into a small word before the noun. So when an English speaker hears “I read book,” it sounds unfinished, like something is missing.

Articles rarely stop people from understanding you. But getting them right makes your English sound much more natural, especially in writing.

Question 1: Can I count it?

Countable nouns can be one or many: an apple, two apples. Uncountable nouns usually can’t: water, advice, information, homework. You never say “an advice” or “a homework.”

  • Singular countable nouns need something in front: a, the, my, this, and so on.
  • Plural and uncountable nouns can stand alone when you mean “in general”: “I like dogs.” “Water is important.”

Question 2: Does the listener know which one?

This is the heart of it.

  • A / an = one of many, not specific yet, or new information. “I saw a cat.”
  • The = we both know which one. “The cat was sleeping on my car.”

Notice the pattern: first mention usually gets a, and later mentions get the, because now the listener knows which cat.

Question 3: Is it the only one?

If there is only one in the situation, use the: the sun, the internet, the president, the door (in a room with one door). Everyone knows which one, because there’s only one.

A or an?

This depends on the sound, not the letter. Use an before a vowel sound: an apple, an hour (the h is silent). Use a before a consonant sound: a university (it starts with a “you” sound), a one-way ticket.

Common article mistakes (and fixes)

  • “I am student.” → “I am a student.” (jobs and roles need a)
  • “I go to the school by bus.” → “I go to school by bus.” (school, bed, work, and church often have no article when you mean the normal activity)
  • “The life is hard.” → “Life is hard.” (general ideas usually have no article)
  • “Can you give me an information?” → “Can you give me some information?”
  • “I live in the Korea.” → “I live in Korea.” (most country names have no article, but the United States, the UK, and the Philippines do)

A 3-step check you can use when writing

  1. Find every noun in your sentence.
  2. Is it singular and countable? It needs something in front.
  3. Does the reader know which one? Yes → the. No → a/an.

How to get better at articles naturally

Rules help when you write, but in speaking you won’t have time to think. The long-term fix is lots of reading and listening. When you read, notice article + noun chunks: have a look, at the moment, in the morning, once a week. Learn them as whole phrases. Over time, “I read book” will start to sound wrong to you too, and that’s when you know it’s working.
